    03 About the cook

    Naomi Kingbird. A Montana kitchen, kept working.

    Naomi is a Blackfeet woman living in Montana. She grew up around family kitchens, simple ingredients, garden harvests, cast-iron pans, pantry shelves and preserved food, and the habit of making the most of whatever was there. She never trained as a cook. She learned what to save, what to stretch, what to put up for winter, and how to treat plain food properly.

    "Some of these methods are old and some are new. The purpose is the same: cook well, waste less, and keep useful kitchen knowledge from being forgotten."

    05 Questions

    Before you set the table.

    Do I need to be an experienced cook?

    No. Chapter one covers ten kitchen habits (broth from bones, rendered fat, a bean pot, dried herbs, a starter jar and a reusable pickle brine among them), and everything after that builds on those.

    Are these authentic traditional recipes?

    These are home-kitchen recipes as Naomi cooks them, shaped by family practice and by a long mixing of Native, homestead and frontier cooking across Montana. They are everyday food, not ceremonial food. Nothing here is presented as sacred, restricted or tribally certified, and no community's protected practices are included.

    How do I get it?

    Right after checkout you're taken to a download page for the 520-page PDF. It opens on any phone, tablet or computer, and prints cleanly on ordinary paper.

    Are the dollar figures guaranteed savings?

    No. They're U.S. planning ranges, not quotes from a particular store. Prices change with location and season. Metric conversions and Celsius temperatures are included.

    There are chapters on wild food and game. Is that for me?

    Only if it suits where you live. Those chapters assume you already hunt, fish or gather legally in your area, and every one of their recipes works just as well with meat and fruit from a shop. Hunting, fishing and foraging rules are yours to check locally.
